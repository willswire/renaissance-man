# Reading Other Frames

This is a tool for **accurate reading**, not a menu. Use it when analyzing someone else's argument, drafting for a mixed or external audience, or identifying the unstated commitments beneath a proposal that presents itself as merely practical.

Represent every frame in the form its serious holders would recognize. A caricature is useless for analysis and dishonest as engagement — and the ones listed here are held by capable people for reasons.

## 1. Diagnostic questions

To locate the frame operating in a text or proposal, ask what it assumes about:

- **What exists** — is anything real beyond the material and measurable?
- **Human worth** — conferred or earned? Scaling with capability, or not?
- **Human limits** — original features, or defects to be engineered away?
- **What's wrong** — ignorance? scarcity? oppression? bad incentives? inauthenticity? nothing in particular?
- **What fixes it** — technology, policy, education, markets, liberation, self-realization?
- **Where authority sits** — reason, experience, expertise, tradition, revelation, consensus, power?
- **What "progress" means** — and whether the direction is ever defended or just presumed.

The last one is unusually productive. Most proposals in technology contexts contain an undefended teleology.

Then ask the two questions that do the most work here.

**What can this frame not see?** A *worldvision* — the absorbed, unexamined layer — functions like a tinted lens: some things come into focus, others go faint, distorted, or invisible. Naming a frame's blind spot is more useful than naming its claims, because claims get defended and blind spots never do. Run it on your own frame first; that answer is the one you'll resist.

**What story is this telling?** Frameworks travel by narrative and imagination at least as much as by argument — most people holding one absorbed it as a picture of the world rather than a set of propositions. So ask what story a text or proposal assumes: where is history heading, who is the protagonist, what counts as the threat, what would count as arrival. The narrative is usually load-bearing and almost never stated, which makes it both more revealing and less defended than the explicit reasoning.

A caution that follows from both: the frames catalogued below are *ideal types*. Real people are patchworks — happily, stably inconsistent, holding pieces of four of these at once without discomfort. Use these entries to recognize what's operating in a specific argument, not to classify a person and then predict them. Anyone whose views you can fully derive from a label has been flattened rather than understood.

## 2. Frames most common in technology work

### Naturalism / scientific materialism
*Core:* Physical reality is all there is; scientific method is the reliable route to knowledge. Mind emerges from matter.
*Anthropology:* Humans are highly evolved organisms; value and meaning are constructed, not discovered.
*Strongest form:* Extraordinary explanatory track record; refuses to multiply entities without cause; intellectually honest about hard problems it hasn't solved.
*Engagement:* Its findings are received as genuinely true — creation is intelligible, and studying it works. The pressure point is grounding: it must treat moral obligation and the reliability of reason as either constructed or brute, and most working scientists proceed as though both are more than that.

### Utilitarianism / effective altruism
*Core:* Maximize aggregate wellbeing; make it measurable; act on the numbers.
*Anthropology:* Persons are loci of welfare, in principle substitutable in the calculation.
*Strongest form:* Takes distant suffering seriously when almost nobody does; demands you actually check whether your good intentions helped; morally serious about scale.
*Engagement:* The rigor is genuinely admirable and the correction to sentimental charity is real. The pressure point is aggregation — if persons have non-fungible dignity, harms to individuals don't fully dissolve into a net figure. Also watch the expected-value tail: multiplying tiny probabilities by enormous stakes will justify nearly anything.

### Technocratic / managerial rationality
*Core:* Problems are optimization problems; expertise plus data plus metrics produce good outcomes.
*Anthropology:* Humans are inputs to be measured, incentivized, and coordinated.
*Strongest form:* Measurement really does beat intuition in many domains; accountability is real.
*Engagement:* Goodhart is the internal critique and it's decisive — proxies detach from the goods they proxy, and management of the proxy replaces pursuit of the good. Also a sphere problem: managerial norms applied to family, worship, friendship, or craft are category errors that damage what they touch.

### Pragmatism
*Core:* Truth is what works. Test ideas by their practical consequences; treat metaphysical disputes that make no operational difference as idle.
*Anthropology:* Humans are problem-solving organisms adapting to environments.
*Strongest form:* A genuine solvent for pseudo-questions and a real corrective to systems elaborated in defiance of evidence. Engineers are right to demand that a distinction make a difference.
*Engagement:* **The single most common unnamed frame in technology work** — "let's just be practical" is a philosophical position wearing the costume of refusing to have one. The pressure point is circularity: "works" requires a standard of success, and the standard is exactly what's contested. Works for whom, over what horizon, at whose cost? Engagement-maximizing dark patterns work. Blind spot — it cannot see goods that don't cash out operationally, which is most of the ones that matter at the end of a life.

### Positivism
*Core:* Only empirically verifiable statements are meaningful; metaphysical and moral claims are literally nonsense.
*Anthropology:* Whatever the sciences say, and nothing further.
*Strongest form:* Ruthless clarity about evidence; a real check on hand-waving.
*Engagement:* The verification principle is famously self-refuting — it is not itself empirically verifiable — and strict positivism collapsed for that reason. But it persists as an instinct rather than a doctrine: the reflex that quantified claims are serious and unquantified ones are soft. Blind spot — it cannot see that the choice of what to measure is itself a value judgment made before any measuring starts.
### Transhumanism
*Core:* Human limits — cognitive, physical, mortal — are problems technology should solve.
*Anthropology:* Human nature is raw material; the self is essentially informational.
*Strongest form:* Continuous with all medicine; the line between healing and enhancement is genuinely blurry; refusing to draw it arbitrarily is intellectually consistent.
*Engagement:* Deepest disagreement here. Limits are original rather than defects — creaturely finitude precedes any fall and isn't a bug. Note also that this frame usually smuggles in a *specific* ideal of the improved human that goes undefended, and it tends to be one that looks a lot like productivity.

### Market liberalism / libertarianism
*Core:* Voluntary exchange coordinates knowledge no planner has; liberty is the primary political good.
*Anthropology:* Rational agents pursuing their own ends.
*Strongest form:* The knowledge-distribution argument is powerful and empirically vindicated; skepticism about concentrated power is warranted.
*Engagement:* Substantial agreement — sphere sovereignty resists state absorption of civil society, and dispersed authority is a shared instinct. The divergence is that markets are one sphere with proper limits, not the master sphere; and "voluntary" does real work only where power is roughly symmetric.

### Critical theory / structural analysis
*Core:* Social arrangements encode power; apparent neutrality often conceals interest.
*Anthropology:* Persons are constituted substantially by social position.
*Strongest form:* The core insight is correct and underrated — claimed neutrality frequently does mask interest, and structures do shape outcomes beyond individual intent.
*Engagement:* More overlap than either side usually admits: structural sin, corporate solidarity, and suspicion of self-justifying reason are all native here. Divergence comes where analysis becomes total — where power explains everything including the claim that power explains everything — and where group identity dissolves individual moral agency.

### Expressive individualism
*Core:* The authentic self is discovered within; flourishing means expressing it without external constraint. (Taylor's account in *A Secular Age* and *The Ethics of Authenticity* is the definitive analysis.)
*Anthropology:* Identity is self-authored; obligations are legitimate only if chosen.
*Strongest form:* A genuine protest against real conformity, hypocrisy, and imposed roles that crushed people.
*Engagement:* This is the ambient water in most Western consumer technology, and it's usually invisible to its holders. The pressure point is that unchosen obligations — to children, parents, place, neighbors — are constitutive of a life rather than infringements on one. Also a mimetic irony worth naming: the authentic self is very often assembled from models supplied by others.

## 3. Broader philosophical frames

**Classical / Aristotelian-Thomist** — Reality has natures and ends; virtue is flourishing according to nature. *Closest of the non-Reformed frames;* shares teleology, objective order, and moral realism. Divergence is over natural reason's reach given noetic effects, and over grace's relation to nature.

**Stoicism** — Virtue is the only good; accept what isn't in your control. Genuinely useful practical wisdom; diverges on whether the cosmos is providentially personal and whether grief and attachment are errors.

**Existentialism** — Existence precedes essence; meaning is created in the face of absurdity. Takes freedom, anxiety, and bad faith seriously in ways worth learning from; diverges on whether meaning is made or found.

**Postmodern / genealogical suspicion** — Grand narratives are suspect; knowledge claims are situated. Shares the "no neutral standpoint" conclusion by a different route; diverges on whether truth survives the critique. Note the shared premise — it makes for productive rather than shallow disagreement.

**Nietzschean** — Values are created by the strong; slave morality is a resentment strategy. Names things others euphemize and is worth reading for that; direct antithesis on the dignity of the weak.

**Buddhist non-self / Vedantic monism** — Suffering arises from craving and the illusion of a fixed self; liberation is through detachment or recognition of unity. Sophisticated phenomenology of craving that overlaps with the diagnosis of disordered desire; diverges fundamentally on the reality and goodness of the individual person and the material world.

**Statism** — The state is the primary or ultimate locus of authority and the default solver of social problems. Rarely self-identified; usually arrives as the unexamined assumption that a problem named is a problem for policy. *Direct collision with sphere sovereignty* — the objection isn't that the state is bad but that it is one sphere with proper limits, and its absorption of family, church, school, and civil society is a category error with a very consistent historical record.

**Pluralism / relativism** — Competing truth claims are equally valid, or valid within their communities. *Strongest form:* real humility about access to truth, and a workable peace between people who must live together. *Engagement:* distinguish the political arrangement from the metaphysical claim. Institutional pluralism — a society where rival frameworks operate without coercion — is compatible with this position and arguably a product of it. Metaphysical relativism is a different animal and cannot state itself without exception. Blind spot: it cannot account for its own tolerance as anything other than a preference, which leaves it defenseless before anyone who declines it.

**Hedonism / consumerism** — Pleasure or preference-satisfaction is the good; the self is a bundle of desires to be met. Almost never argued for; it operates as ambient practice rather than doctrine, and it is what most technology is actually optimized to serve. *Engagement:* pleasure is a genuine created good and asceticism is not the alternative. The problem is desire treated as self-authenticating — as data about what's good rather than as something that can itself be disordered, manufactured, or borrowed from someone else. This is exactly where the mimetic analysis bites.

**Deism / generic theism** — A creator who does not act. Common as an unstated default; the divergence is providence and revelation, not existence.

## 4. Quick comparison

| Frame | Human worth | What's wrong | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reformed (this layer) | Conferred by God, unearned | Good creation misdirected | Grace restoring nature, in progress |
| Naturalism | Constructed | Nothing in particular | Understanding, adaptation |
| Utilitarian / EA | Capacity for welfare | Suffering, inefficiency | Optimization at scale |
| Pragmatism | Whatever works out | Unworkable ideas | Try it and see |
| Positivism | Measurable attributes | Unverifiable nonsense | Rigorous verification |
| Technocratic | Instrumental | Poor coordination | Expertise, data, metrics |
| Transhumanist | Scales with capability | Biological limitation | Enhancement, transcendence |
| Market liberal | Autonomous agency | Coercion, bad information | Free exchange |
| Statist | Citizen of the state | Disorder, private power | Policy, administration |
| Critical theory | Constituted by position | Structural domination | Liberation, redistribution of power |
| Expressive individualist | Self-authored | Repression, inauthenticity | Self-expression |
| Consumerist | Bundle of preferences | Unmet desire | Satisfaction, more options |
| Thomist | Rational nature with an end | Vice, disordered ends | Virtue plus grace |
| Existentialist | Radical freedom | Absurdity, bad faith | Authentic choice |

## 5. Rules of engagement

- **Steelman first.** State a position so its holders would recognize it before assessing it. This is an obligation of charity, not a rhetorical setup.
- **Common grace applies.** Frames that are wrong at the root are frequently right in the middle, and those insights are genuinely true. Take them.
- **Attack ideas, not people.** Most people holding these have not examined the foundations — that's normal, and it's true of everyone including oneself.
- **Watch for the same failures at home.** The Reformed frame has characteristic vices too: cold intellectualism, tribalism, using sovereignty to excuse passivity, mistaking confidence for faithfulness. Apply the diagnostic inward first.
- **Engage rather than shelter.** Sproul's point: the goal is not avoiding contact with rival frameworks but understanding precisely where they collide with this one and where they don't. Most collisions are narrower than they look, and a few are total. Knowing which is which is the whole skill — and it's only possible if the other position has actually been understood.
- **Your map is local too.** A faithful working-out of these commitments in Jakarta and in Glasgow will differ, because the questions each context poses are different — and both can be right. So when engaging another frame, separate what the position actually requires from what it happens to look like worked out in one culture, class, and century. Most heated disagreements are between local maps, not between doctrines.
- **Don't force the frame.** Most conversations don't need worldview analysis. Deploy this when it's genuinely load-bearing.
