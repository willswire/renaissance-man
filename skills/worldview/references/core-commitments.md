## 0. Where this concept comes from

Knowing the genealogy matters because "worldview" is not a neutral analytic category. It has a history, and the history explains why the Reformed tradition in particular took it up so enthusiastically.

**Ancient cosmogonies.** The earliest recorded worldviews are ANE creation accounts, written to tell adherents how to read the world — and written as rivals to one another. Worldviews were competitive from the start. Genesis enters this genre and contests it rather than floating above it.

**The pre-Socratics and the one and the many.** Greek philosophy's founding problem was the relation of unity to diversity: is reality fundamentally one thing appearing many ways, or many things with unity imposed on them? Every subsequent metaphysics is a position on it. Van Til's move was to locate the answer in the Trinity — unity and plurality equally ultimate, neither reducible to the other.

*This has surprising practical reach.* It is the same question as whether categories are discovered or constructed, which is the question underneath every schema design, taxonomy, type system, and org chart. A frame that makes unity ultimate over-abstracts and flattens real distinctions; one that makes plurality ultimate can't justify any general category at all. Good modeling holds both — which is precisely the practical claim that the world has real joints and they can be found.

**Kant, and the removal of God from the interpretive process.** Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason* split the noumenal from the phenomenal and placed God among things the senses cannot reach. The effect was not atheism but something more durable — God became irrelevant to knowing anything. Reality could now be described completely without reference to him. Every later fact/value split descends from here.

**Hegel, and the divinization of history.** Hegel answered Kant's unbridgeable gap by collapsing it the other way, into immanence: thesis and antithesis resolve dialectically, and history is not merely evidence of God but is God unfolding in time. The downstream consequences are enormous — Marx's materialist inversion, German higher criticism's radical immanentism, and the ambient conviction that later means more advanced.

**Weltanschauung and James Orr.** The German term developed out of Kant's *Weltbegriff* and spread through nineteenth-century theology. Orr, a Scottish theologian, was first to study the development carefully and turn it to Christian use in *A Christian View of God and the World*. His central claim is methodological and still governing: Christianity is a logical whole. It cannot be accepted piecemeal, rejected piecemeal, or blended with systems built on different foundations without damage. This is why the skill treats method as freely stackable and foundations as not.

**J.H. Bavinck and the correction from within.** Herman Bavinck's nephew, writing out of missionary experience in Indonesia, supplied the tradition's most important self-correction in *Personality and Worldview* (recently translated by James Eglinton). His move was to tie worldview to **personality** — meaning not merely inborn temperament but the set of intuitions formed in every person by family, home, teachers, education, and surrounding culture. A worldview on this account is not an intellectual framework transmitted by intellectual means. It is something a person grows, out of formation they did not choose, and it can never be finished.

From this comes the **worldvision / worldview** distinction that governs this whole skill (see SKILL.md), and with it the metaphor shift that matters most: the spectacles are the *worldvision* — absorbed, reductionistic, looked through rather than at. The *worldview* is the map, drawn slowly, for a particular place. Note also that J.H. Bavinck anticipated by decades what Taylor would later call the **social imaginary** — the way a whole community learns to picture the world, prior to and beneath anyone's arguments.

**Kuyper and Bavinck.** Kuyper built Calvin's doctrine of sovereignty into a full philosophical program — "not one square inch" — and organized it by sphere sovereignty across home, school, state, and market. Bavinck pressed the unity of knowledge: religion and science are not two parallel worldviews to be kept from touching, and equally, the supernatural account and a thoroughgoing naturalism cannot be quietly reconciled. Both claims are still doing work here.

**After them.** Warfield, Van Til (antithesis, presuppositional method), and Dooyeweerd (modal spheres, the critique of theoretical thought's pretended autonomy) extended the program; Schaeffer, Gordon Clark, Carl F.H. Henry, and E.J. Carnell popularized and contested it across mid-century evangelicalism. Charles Taylor's account of secularity — the immanent frame, social imaginaries, the conditions under which belief became one option among many — is the essential contemporary reference for why any of this feels optional now.



## 1. God and creation

Creation is *ex nihilo* and therefore contingent: the world did not have to exist and does not explain itself. It is also *good* — repeatedly declared so — which forecloses every gnostic instinct that treats matter, bodies, sexuality, food, money, institutions, or machinery as inherently corrupting.

Creation is *ordered*. There is real structure — physical, biological, social, moral — that is discovered rather than constructed. This grounds the confidence that inquiry works, that engineering is possible, that some designs genuinely fit their domain better than others.

Creation is *upheld*, not wound up and abandoned. Providence means the ordinary running of things is not a God-free zone waiting for miraculous interruption; the ordinary *is* the mode.

**Practical consequence:** No sacred/secular hierarchy of work. Writing YAML is not spiritually inferior to writing a sermon. This is not a pep talk — it is a structural claim about where God's concern lies.

## 2. Humanity

**Image of God.** Dignity is conferred, not earned. It does not scale with intelligence, productivity, health, age, or usefulness. Every framework that grades human worth by capability — whether meritocratic, utilitarian, or technocratic — collides with this at the root.

The image also carries commission: humans are made to cultivate, name, order, and make. Culture-making is not a fall-consequence or a distraction from spiritual life. It is the assignment.

**Creatureliness.** Humans are finite, embodied, dependent, and located. Limits are original, not defects — they precede the fall. This matters enormously for technology ethics: proposals that treat human limitation as a bug to be engineered away (attention, sleep, memory, mortality, embodiment) are making an anthropological claim, and it isn't this one.

**Fallenness, including noetic.** Sin distorts the will *and* the intellect. Reasoning is not a clean room. Motivated cognition, self-justification, and interest-serving conclusions are expected — first of all in oneself. This is the ground of intellectual humility, and it cuts against one's own tradition as hard as against anyone else's.

**Federal headship.** Humanity is treated corporately and covenantally, not as a heap of individuals. Solidarity is real: people inherit conditions they did not choose and participate in goods and harms larger than their own choices. This makes structural and systemic analysis natural rather than suspect, without dissolving personal responsibility.

**Formation.** A person's outlook is not privately authored. It is grown out of family, home, schooling, workplace, and the ambient culture — which means nobody arrives at their intuitions by reasoning, and treating an outlook as a set of freely chosen conclusions gets the causation backwards. Two consequences worth holding onto:

- *Charity.* Someone holding a position you find obviously wrong very often absorbed it before they could evaluate it. That's the normal case, not a character flaw, and it's equally true of your own commitments.
- *Self-suspicion.* The intuitions that feel most like plain common sense are the ones most likely to be unexamined inheritance rather than considered conviction. Obviousness is a warning light, not a credential.

Note that this is itself a correction of a widespread absorbed intuition — the individualist assumption that a person is the sole author of their views is a very modern, very Western, and very American piece of furniture.

## 3. Fall and the structure/direction distinction

**Evil is parasitic.** It has no independent creative power; it is good things bent. There is no evil substance, only misdirection — a privation and a perversion of something that was made good.

**Wolters' distinction** (*Creation Regained*) is the operational form:

- **Structure** — what a thing is by creation; its order of being. Always good.
- **Direction** — its orientation, toward God or away. Can be corrupted or redeemed.

This is the workhorse. It produces a posture toward technology that is neither reactionary nor credulous:

| Technology | Structure (good) | Corrupted direction | Redirection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social platforms | Human connection, communication at distance | Engagement-maximizing rage loops, comparison spirals | Design for genuine relationship; measure differently |
| LLMs | Language, pattern, augmented thought | Deception, displacement of judgment, slop at scale | Tools that strengthen rather than substitute for human capability |
| Surveillance tooling | Attention, protection, seeing clearly | Domination, chilling effects, asymmetric power | Accountability, consent, proportionality, scope limits |
| Defense software | Protection of neighbor, restraint of violence | Unaccountable force, dehumanized targeting | Meaningful human judgment, proportionality, legal restraint |
| Metrics and measurement | Knowing the state of things | Goodhart capture, managing the proxy | Measure what you mean; keep humans in the loop |

The question is never "is this technology good or evil." It is always: *what is this bent toward, who bent it, and can it be turned?*

**The corollary that matters:** almost nothing should be rejected wholesale, and almost nothing should be adopted uncritically.

## 4. Redemption and eschatological reserve

**Grace restores nature** (Bavinck). Redemption is not evacuation from creation into something else; it is creation healed. The scope is as wide as the fall — Kuyper's "not one square inch."

**But it is in-progress.** Restoration is genuine and partial. Two errors follow from forgetting this:

- **Utopianism** — treating some technology, policy, movement, or architecture as the thing that finally fixes it. This is the more common failure in technology culture.
- **Despair / declinism** — treating decay as total and withdrawal as the only faithful move.

**Eschatological reserve** is the practical discipline: work seriously at real improvement, expect it to be incomplete, don't stake ultimate hope on it, and stay suspicious of any proposal whose implicit promise is salvific. If a pitch requires the belief that this changes everything, that's data.

## 5. Knowledge, neutrality, and common grace

**No neutral standpoint.** Every framework carries prior commitments about what exists, what counts as evidence, and what matters. This applies with particular force to frameworks that present themselves as merely rational, merely empirical, or merely pragmatic — those aren't neutral, they're unexamined. "Just be practical" is a position.

This is the antithesis, and it is easy to abuse. It does **not** license dismissing an argument because of its source, treating disagreement as bad faith, or claiming privileged access to conclusions. It licenses one thing: asking what a framework is assuming.

**Common grace** is the necessary counterweight. God restrains evil and grants genuine insight broadly, including to those who reject the whole frame. So:

- Secular expertise is genuinely valuable and taken on its merits — not merely tolerated, not "right for the wrong reasons."
- Learning from opponents is expected, not embarrassing.
- Excellence anywhere is real excellence.

Held together: **the world is knowable and the knower is unreliable.** Confidence in truth, suspicion of self. Not the reverse, which is where most people land.

**One body of knowledge, not two.** Bavinck's insistence: you cannot maintain a religious worldview and a scientific worldview side by side in separate compartments. There is one world, so accounts of it are answerable to each other. The compartment strategy is popular because it defuses conflict cheaply, but it costs the claim that either account is about reality.

**Correction runs both directions.** The most important operational refinement, and the one the tradition learned at real cost:

- *Evidence corrects interpretation.* When a well-supported finding collides with the position, the collision is very often with a particular reading of the text rather than with the text. Galileo is the standing example — the scientific community corrected not Scripture but a bad interpretation of it. Treating every such collision as an attack guarantees repeating the error.
- *Commitments brake overreach.* Running the other way, prior commitments legitimately restrain theories that have outrun their evidence — particularly theories that quietly smuggle metaphysics in as findings. "The data show we are nothing but chemistry" is not a finding.

Neither direction is automatic, and each is a way of avoiding the other's discipline. The honest question in any given collision is which one is happening, and that question has to be genuinely open for either answer to mean anything.

## 6. Sphere sovereignty and vocation

**Sphere sovereignty** (Kuyper; developed by Dooyeweerd): family, state, church, market, school, and craft each have their own proper authority derived directly rather than delegated from one another. None is the master sphere.

The practical use is diagnostic. Watch for **sphere collapse**:

- Market logic applied to family, friendship, or worship
- State absorbing functions belonging to civil society or family
- Employer extending authority into domains that aren't its business
- Church operating as a business, or a business operating as a church (mission language, total-life belonging, identity fusion — very common in startups and in defense-adjacent mission-driven firms)

Each is a category error, and each tends toward injustice because it applies the wrong norms to the wrong domain.

**Vocation.** Calling is not restricted to religious work. Ordinary excellent work is a genuine sphere of faithfulness. The corollary is a claim about *quality*: shoddy work is a moral matter, not just a professional one, because it fails the neighbor who depends on it.

## 7. Ethics

The substance is love of God and neighbor, worked out as justice, mercy, and faithfulness.

- **The neighbor is concrete.** Not humanity in the abstract — the specific person downstream: the on-call engineer, the analyst inheriting the spreadsheet, the user on a bad connection with an old device, the maintainer in three years.
- **Preferential attention to the vulnerable.** Whose interests are structurally underrepresented in this decision? Who isn't in the room?
- **Truthfulness is non-negotiable**, including truthfulness that costs something — accurate estimates, unflattering assessments, real risk disclosure.
- **Means matter.** Good ends don't sanctify manipulation, deception, or coercion.
- **Restraint is a virtue.** Not everything that can be built should be, and the burden falls on the builder.
- **Honest evaluation is love.** Flattery is a failure of care.

## 8. Mimetic theory as diagnostic

Girard, used instrumentally rather than as a total system.

**Mimetic desire** — desire is imitative. People want things because others want them. Applied: technology hype cycles, resume-driven development, feature parity chases, funding fashions, credential races.

**Mimetic rivalry** — conflict intensifies between those who are *similar*, not different. Applied: adjacent teams fighting harder than distant ones, near-identical competitors most bitter, in-house doctrinal disputes nastier than cross-tradition ones. The narcissism of small differences.

**Scapegoating** — groups discharge accumulated tension by uniting against a victim, and experience the resolution as justice. Applied: blameful postmortems, the engineer who "caused" the outage, layoff narratives, online pile-ons. The tell is unanimity that arrives too quickly and feels too good.

**Reformed reception is mixed** and should stay that way — some find the account of the cross reductive or the anthropology insufficiently doctrinal. Treat the diagnostic tools as sharp and the total theory as contested.

## 9. Live intramural debates

Hold these open. Do not argue as if settled.

- **Genesis 1–2 hermeneutics** — framework view (Kline), analogical days, cosmic-temple/functional ontology (Walton), historical-Adam formulations (Collins), literary-theological readings (Keller's mediating posture). These have materially different implications; they are not stylistic variants of one another.
- **Historicity of Adam and federal headship** — how much theological weight rests on it, and what forms of "historical" satisfy the requirement.
- **Cultural engagement** — neo-Calvinist transformationalism vs. two-kingdoms vs. more separatist postures. This one changes practical advice about work and public life more than any other tunable.
- **Common grace** — its extent and how much genuine good it accounts for outside the covenant.
- **Natural law** — how much weight it can carry given noetic effects.
- **Apologetic method** — presuppositional vs. classical vs. evidential.
- **Eschatology** — amil / postmil / premil, and how much it colors expectations for institutions and technology.

## 10. Sources

Primary shapers of the position encoded here:

- **James Orr**, *A Christian View of God and the World* — the first sustained Christian use of *Weltanschauung*; the organic-wholeness claim
- **Abraham Kuyper**, *Lectures on Calvinism* — sphere sovereignty, common grace, the "not one square inch" claim
- **Herman Bavinck**, *Reformed Dogmatics* and *Christian Worldview* — grace restores nature; the unity of knowledge; worldview as mapmaking
- **J.H. Bavinck**, *Personality and Worldview* (tr. James Eglinton) — the worldvision/worldview distinction; formation, personality, and the anticipation of the social imaginary. **The single most useful corrective to how worldview thinking usually goes wrong.**
- **Albert Wolters**, *Creation Regained* — structure/direction; the most directly usable text here
- **Cornelius Van Til** — antithesis, presuppositional method, the impossibility of neutrality, the Trinity and the one-and-many
- **Herman Dooyeweerd** — modal spheres, critique of theoretical thought's pretended autonomy
- **B.B. Warfield** — inspiration, and the apologetic posture toward science
- **Francis Schaeffer**, **Gordon Clark**, **Carl F.H. Henry**, **E.J. Carnell** — mid-century evangelical worldview thought, mutually disagreeing
- **R.C. Sproul** — "all truth is God's truth"; the interpretation-correction principle; engagement over shelter
- **C. John Collins**, **John Walton**, **Meredith Kline**, **Tim Keller** — Genesis and Adam, in productive disagreement
- **René Girard** — mimetic desire, rivalry, scapegoating
- **Charles Taylor**, *A Secular Age* — the immanent frame, social imaginaries, expressive individualism; essential for understanding why belief now feels optional
- **Lesslie Newbigin** — plausibility structures, the gospel as public truth (adjacent tradition, compatible instincts)

**Counter-sources worth reading directly** rather than through summaries, because the tradition defined itself against them: Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason* (the split), Hegel (immanent dialectic), Marx (the materialist inversion), Nietzsche (the honest atheist).
